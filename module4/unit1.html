Exercise 1: Examples of "Knowing" Versus "Having Information About"

A second language in which you are fluent: knowing.

The content of a television news programme: either, depending on if you've watched it, personally.

A close friend: having information about.

A company's annual report: either, depending on if you've read it, personally.

Your close friend's partner whom you have yet to meet: having information about.

The weather on the other side of the world: having information about.

The weather where you are now: knowing.

Exercise 2: The Difference Between "Knowing" Versus "Having Information About"

"Knowing" a propositional statement means that you assert that you believe it to be factually true.

"Having information about" a propositional statement means that you're aware of factual statements without personal knowledge of them and/or without any automatic assertion of their factual truth.
